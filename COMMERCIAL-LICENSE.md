## When you need a commercial licence

The AGPL-3.0 has one widely-quoted catch: any derivative work, **and
any network service that exposes the software's functionality to
users**, must itself be released under the AGPL and provide its
complete source code to those users. That's by design — it stops
others from running the app as a paid hosted service while keeping
their own improvements private.

You will probably want a commercial licence if you want to:

- Embed GPSinfo (or any of its modules / packages) in a **closed-source
  Android, web, embedded or in-vehicle product**.
- Bundle GPSinfo into a **proprietary SDK** sold to third parties.
- Build a **white-label fork** with your own branding and ship it under
  a non-AGPL licence.
- Run a **proprietary paid hosting service** that exposes any of
  GPSinfo's code paths to its users without itself being AGPL'd.
- Receive **dedicated support** or **custom feature development**
  outside the public roadmap.

You do **not** need a commercial licence if you:

- Run the app on your own phone (any purpose, including for-profit).
- Fork the repo, modify it, and ship the AGPL-licensed result yourself.
- Build a service whose source you're willing to release under the
  AGPL.
- Translate, localise, or contribute back patches.
